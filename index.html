I am a pharmacy-trained biomedical scientist with research interests in cardiovascular and metabolic physiology, chronic intermittent hypoxia, neuroinflammation, and the central regulation of cardiovascular function.

I earned my Bachelor of Pharmacy at Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology in Ghana in 2014 and completed my PhD in Biomedical Science at the University of North Texas Health Science Center in 2025. I joined Texas A&M University Health Science Center as a Postdoctoral Scientist in October 2025.

My research publications examine chronic intermittent hypoxia, hypertension, sex hormones, neuroinflammation, and activity-related changes in central autonomic regions. My current work also includes analysis of human data from the NIH All of Us Research Program.

Teaching and mentorship are important parts of my professional identity. My experience includes teaching graduate physiology and pharmacology, supporting students through tutoring and office hours, providing peer mentorship, and mentoring undergraduate summer research interns. My CV identifies me as a CIRTL Associate and includes training in evidence-based undergraduate teaching.

Chronic Intermittent Hypoxia & Cardiovascular Regulation

My research explores chronic intermittent hypoxia in relation to hypertension and central autonomic regulation, including how responses differ with sex hormones and ovarian hormone loss. My publications include work on hypoxia-induced hypertension and region-specific changes in ΔFosB expression in female rats.

Neuroinflammation & Brain Function

My published work includes quantitative and morphological analyses of microglia in brain regions linked to cognitive function, as well as a review of nitric oxide and inflammation in hypoxia-associated hypertension and cognitive impairment.

Metabolic Health & Emerging Research Interests

I am interested in how dietary and metabolic conditions interact with intermittent hypoxia to influence cardiometabolic outcomes. My developing interests include the renin–angiotensin system, oxidative stress, transcriptomics, and biomarkers of treatment response.

Teaching & Scientific Communication

I value student-centered, culturally responsive teaching and clear scientific communication. Before my doctoral training, I worked as a Medical Science Liaison in diabetes and infections at Biofem Ghana Limited, communicating with physicians and pharmacists through professional development programs and conferences.
